## Overview of the 2021 guidelines

The WHO published updated global air quality guidelines (AQGs) on 22 September 2021,
replacing the 2005 global update. Based on the accumulated evidence that air pollution
harms health at lower concentrations than previously understood, the guideline levels
were lowered for most pollutants. The guidelines are recommendations, not legally
binding limits: countries use them as a reference when setting their own standards.

## Guideline levels for particulate matter

PM2.5 (fine particles, diameter of 2.5 micrometres or less):
- annual mean: 5 µg/m³ (the 2005 guideline was 10 µg/m³)
- 24-hour mean: 15 µg/m³ (the 2005 guideline was 25 µg/m³)

PM10 (particles of 10 micrometres or less):
- annual mean: 15 µg/m³ (the 2005 guideline was 20 µg/m³)
- 24-hour mean: 45 µg/m³ (the 2005 guideline was 50 µg/m³)

The 24-hour guideline levels are defined as the 99th percentile of the annual
distribution of daily means, which corresponds to about 3–4 exceedance days per year.

## Guideline levels for nitrogen dioxide and other gases

Nitrogen dioxide (NO2):
- annual mean: 10 µg/m³ (the 2005 guideline was 40 µg/m³)
- 24-hour mean: 25 µg/m³ (new in 2021)

Other pollutants in the 2021 guidelines: ozone (peak-season mean 60 µg/m³, 8-hour mean
100 µg/m³), sulfur dioxide (24-hour mean 40 µg/m³) and carbon monoxide (24-hour mean
4 mg/m³).

## Interim targets

Because the guideline levels are hard to reach quickly where pollution is high, WHO
also defines interim targets as stepping stones toward the guidelines.

PM2.5 annual mean interim targets: 35, 25, 15 and 10 µg/m³ (targets 1 to 4).
PM2.5 24-hour interim targets: 75, 50, 37.5 and 25 µg/m³.
PM10 annual mean interim targets: 70, 50, 30 and 20 µg/m³.
PM10 24-hour interim targets: 150, 100, 75 and 50 µg/m³.
