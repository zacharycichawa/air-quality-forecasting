## Current EU limit values (Directive 2008/50/EC)

Unlike WHO guidelines, EU limit values are legally binding for member states,
including Poland. When they are exceeded, the authorities must adopt air quality plans,
and the European Commission can start infringement proceedings.

PM10:
- 24-hour limit: 50 µg/m³, not to be exceeded on more than 35 days per calendar year
- annual limit: 40 µg/m³

PM2.5:
- annual limit: 25 µg/m³ (there is no daily PM2.5 limit in the 2008 directive)

Nitrogen dioxide (NO2):
- 1-hour limit: 200 µg/m³, not to be exceeded more than 18 times per calendar year
- annual limit: 40 µg/m³

## Revised directive: stricter limits from 2030

In 2024 the EU adopted a revised ambient air quality directive, Directive (EU)
2024/2881, which brings EU standards closer to the WHO 2021 guidelines. Its new limit
values apply from 2030:

- PM2.5: annual limit 10 µg/m³; 24-hour limit 25 µg/m³, not to be exceeded more than
  18 times per year
- PM10: annual limit 20 µg/m³; 24-hour limit 45 µg/m³, not to be exceeded more than
  18 times per year
- NO2: annual limit 20 µg/m³; 24-hour limit 50 µg/m³ (max 18 exceedances per year);
  1-hour limit 200 µg/m³ (max 3 exceedances per year)

The revised directive also strengthens citizens' rights to access to justice and to
compensation for health damage caused by breaches of the rules.

## How exceedances are counted

A daily (24-hour) mean is usually considered valid only when enough hourly values are
available (this project requires at least 18 valid hours, i.e. 75 % data coverage).
Exceedance days are counted per station and calendar year.
